## Step 1 — `add x` and `add_λ x` (3 sorries)

`KMonoid.addOf` and `KMonoid.addOfCard` are defined; the three closure lemmas are open.

- `addOf_isLSubset`: `0 ∈ add x` via `n = 0` and `z = 0` (`cmul_zero`); closure under finite sums
  by adding the witnesses and using `cmul_add_cmul` on `(m + n : ℕ)`.  Stated as `IsLSubset ℵ₀`
  rather than as a bare submonoid so that `IsLSubset.lmonoid` gives the subtype the `LMonoid ℵ₀`
  instance that steps 2 and 4 need.
- `addOf_isSaturated`: if `a + z = n • x` and `a = b + c`, then `b + (c + z) = n • x`.
  Associativity only.
- `addOfCard_isLSubset`: the `λ⁻`-sum of a family `(y i)` of summands of `λ x` is again a summand
  of `λ x`, because `λ · λ = λ` for infinite `λ` — use `lcmul_lcmul` and `Cardinal.mul_eq_self`.
  This is the one that needs real work; `IsLSubset` has the `lsumOf`-closure field, so the
  witness is `lsumOf` of the witnesses.

Do this step first: everything downstream mentions `addOf`.

## Step 4 — Corollary 4.7(1) (2 sorries)

`corollary_4_7_one_backward` is (iii) ⇒ (i), and needs no realisation theorem: given that every
projective over `R` is a direct sum of finitely generated modules, `V^κ(R)` is `κ`-generated by
`add [R]`, because `V(R) = add [R]`.  Prove it from `corollary_4_5_three` plus
`isOrderUnit_unitClass` (§2.2).

`corollary_4_7_one_forward` is (i) ⇒ (ii), and uses **axiom A5**, `bergmanDicksData` in
`KappaMonoid/Axioms.lean`.  The chain:

1. `add x` is reduced with order-unit `x` — reducedness from `KMonoid.isConical` (Lemma 2.8, which
   holds since `Order.succ κ > ℵ₀`), the order-unit property straight from the definition of
   `addOf`.
2. A5 gives a `BergmanDicksData k ↥(add x)`: a hereditary `k`-algebra `R`, an isomorphism
   `add x ≅ V(R)` presented as a family `P : add x → Type u` of finitely generated projectives,
   and `sumOfFG`.
3. `sumOfFG` has exactly the shape of the `hfg` hypothesis of `corollary_4_5_three`, so it plugs
   in unchanged and makes `V^κ(R)` `ℵ₀⁻`-braided over `V(R) = lambdaSmallPart ℵ₀`.
4. Transport that braiding along the isomorphism of step 2 to get `V^κ(R)` braided over `add x`,
   then apply step 2 of this plan (`isKIso_of_braidedOver_same`).

Step 4.3 is where the work is: turning `P` into a monoid isomorphism
`↥(add x) ≃ ↥(lambdaSmallPart ℵ₀)` means going from `iso_zero`/`iso_add`/`inj`/`surj` to a
statement about `V^κ(R)`-classes, and `Modules.lean`'s `rep`/`mk` API is the way across.  Beware
trap 2 above: `lambdaSmallPart` is a subtype of a subtype.

(ii) ⇒ (iii) is trivial and needs no separate statement.

## Step 5 — Corollary 4.6 (0 sorries — nothing to prove)

Corollary 4.6 is `corollary_4_5_three` plus six citations: weakly semihereditary (Bergman),
one-sided semihereditary (Bergman), exchange (Warfield), semiperfect (Mueller), weakly noetherian
commutative (Hinohara), Bézout with one-sided Krull dimension (McGovern–Puninski–Rothmaler).
Each says *every projective module is a direct sum of finitely generated modules*, which is
`EveryProjectiveIsSumOfFG` in the scaffold; given it, Corollary 4.5(3) concludes.

None of the six is in Mathlib, and none is a monoid-theoretic statement.  **The documented stub in
`Modules.lean` is the correct treatment** — leave it.  `EveryProjectiveIsSumOfFG` exists so the
six implications can be stated the day that module theory lands.

## Step 6 — Example 4.8(1), KRSA ascent (1 sorry)

`krsa_ascent`: if `V^{λ⁻}(C_{λ⁻})` is a free `λ⁻`-monoid on `B` and every module in `C` is a
direct sum of `λ⁻`-small ones, then `V^κ(C)` is the free `κ`-monoid on `B`.

Chain: Theorem 4.3 gives `λ⁻`-braidedness (this is the `hbr` hypothesis, discharged by
`theorem_4_3` in an application); Lemma 3.13(1) — `lemma_3_13_free` in the §3 scaffold — says the
universal `κ`-extension of `F_{λ⁻}(B)` is `F_κ(B)`; step 2 identifies the two.

**Blocked on `SECTION3-PLAN.md` step 2** (`lemma_3_13_free`).  Do §3 first, or carry 3.13(1) as a
hypothesis here.

This is the one worked example of §4 that needs no literature at all — the rest of Examples 4.8
(direct summands of infinite direct sums; the `V(R) ≅ ℕ₀ / ℚ≥0 / ℝ≥0` computations; Dedekind
domains via Steinitz) are either open questions or specialisations of the §3 examples.  The
`ℕ₀ ∪ {∞}`, `ℝ̃≥0` and Diophantine computations they cite are exactly Examples 3.3 and
Proposition 3.14, already scaffolded in `KappaMonoid/Section32.lean`.
