# κ-monoids and infinite direct-sum decompositions, in Lean 4

A complete Lean 4 / Mathlib formalisation of Sections 2–5 of

> Zahra Nazemian and Daniel Smertnig, *A monoid-theoretical approach to infinite direct-sum
> decompositions of modules* (`kappa_monoids.tex`, `kappa_monoids.pdf` in this repository).

Everything is proved: there is no `sorry` and no axiom beyond Lean's own (`propext`,
`Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`).

## What the paper proves

The monoid `V(C)` of isomorphism classes of a class `C` of modules records how finite direct sums
behave. The paper adds an operation for `κ`-indexed direct sums, turning `V(C)` into a
**`κ`-monoid** `V^κ(C)`. A **braiding** property, equivalent to a universal property, shows that
all relations between infinite direct sums are induced by relations between smaller ones. For
projective modules this sharpens Kaplansky's theorem. Over hereditary rings, `V(R)` determines
`V^κ(R)` completely. §5 classifies the two-generated `ℵ₀`-monoids that arise from hereditary rings.

## Reading it against the paper

Start in `KappaMonoid/Paper/`, which is written to be read with the PDF open.

- **`Section2.lean`, `Section3.lean`, `Section4.lean`**: one entry per numbered item, naming the
  declaration that formalises it. Search for the item: `grep -n "Lemma 3.14" KappaMonoid/Paper/*.lean`.
  Each file ends with what is not formalised, and why.
- **`Section5.lean`**: restates every result of §5 in the paper's own terms. The standing
  assumption ("`H` is a non-cyclic `ℵ₀`-monoid generated by `x₁, x₂`") is the structure
  `Setting5`. `Section5Extra.lean` has the unnumbered claims of §5.
- **`Definition21.lean`, `Definition218.lean`**: the definitions of `κ`-monoids and `λ⁻`-monoids
  transcribed word for word, with proofs that they agree with the working definitions.

Throughout the library, docstrings open with the paper reference (**Lemma 3.14(2)**, …) and most
carry a short `Paper proof:` paragraph.

## Differences from the paper

None of these changes what is proved. Each is explained in the docstring of the declaration
concerned.

- **Left modules.** Mathlib's modules are left modules, and the paper's are right modules. So the
  paper's "right hereditary" is `IsLeftHereditary` here. An unqualified "hereditary" is
  `IsHereditary`, which means both sides.
- **One notion of summation.** A κ-monoid is set up as a λ⁻-monoid with λ = κ⁺. Sums are taken
  over any index type of the right size, not over the cardinal itself.
- **Braidings in normal form.** Definition 3.1 fixes an arbitrary limit well-order. Here braidings
  are indexed by `ι × ℕ`, one ω-block per limit element. Lemma 3.5 (`isBraidedOn_iff_isBraided`)
  proves this is equivalent for every limit well-order.
- **Universes.** A ring, its modules and κ share one universe.
  `IsUniversalKExtension` takes the universe of its test objects as a parameter. The main results
  hold for every such universe.
- **Two hypotheses the paper leaves implicit:**
  - κ > ℵ₀ in the converse of Remark 2.19 (`LMonoid.ofCompatible`). At κ = ℵ₀ there is no infinite
    λ < κ.
  - C reduced in the §5 preamble's form `C ∪ {∞}`. The trivial extension exists only for reduced
    C.
- **Places where the formalisation proves more:**
  - Proposition 3.15(2) holds for any system whose solutions are saturated (`prop_3_15_two`).
  - Proposition 5.4's final statement holds for every ring whose projectives are direct sums of
    finitely generated ones.
  - Lemma 3.4(3) holds without its side condition.
  - For λ > ℵ₀, the two middle partitions in Lemma 3.7 can be taken equal.

## What is not formalised

- **Remark 3.18.** Background from the literature; nothing later depends on it.
- **Corollary 4.6**, apart from the hereditary case. The other five ring classes rest on results
  quoted from the literature.
- **Examples 4.8(2) and (5)–(7).** Item (2) is a question. Items (5)–(7) rest on classical
  descriptions of `V(R)` quoted from the literature: Bass, Herbera–Příhoda, Levy–Robson.
- **Steinitz's theorem in Examples 4.8(4).** It is quoted. The monoid side is formalised in full.
- **Three unproved remarks:** the remark after Corollary 4.7 (tex 1840), the negative half of the
  caution in Examples 4.8(1), and the motivational remark before Proposition 2.16 (which follows
  from `prop_2_16_iff`).

## Other results proved here

These have no κ-monoid content and are not in Mathlib.

- **The Bergman–Dicks realisation theorem** (`Bergman/`). The ring is presented by universal
  idempotents and isomorphisms. Its `V` is computed with Bergman's theorem on coproducts of rings
  (*Modules over coproducts of rings*, 1974). Heredity follows from quasi-freeness: idempotents and
  isomorphisms lift along square-zero extensions. `Bergman/Index.lean` maps Bergman's numbered
  results to their declarations.
- **Kaplansky's theorem**: every projective module is a direct sum of countably generated
  projectives.
- **Albrecht's theorem**: over a hereditary ring, every projective module is a direct sum of
  finitely generated projectives.
- **Invariance of infinite rank** for free modules, with no rank condition on the ring.
- **Uniqueness of multiplicities of simple summands**, including infinite multiplicities.
- **The classification of cyclic monoids**: `ℕ₀` or `C_{m,n}`.
- **Hereditary rings** (left, right, two-sided), trace ideals, and some facts on `finsum` and
  cardinal sums.

## Layout

The code is organised by subject, not by paper section. Each directory imports only the ones above
it in this table; `scripts/check_layering.sh` enforces that.

| Directory | Contents |
|---|---|
| `ForMathlib/` | general results with no κ-monoids (Kaplansky, Albrecht, …) |
| `Bergman/` | the Bergman–Dicks realisation theorem |
| `Core/` | §2: κ- and λ⁻-monoids, homomorphisms, free and cyclic κ-monoids, order-units |
| `Braiding/` | §3: braidings, universal κ-extensions (Theorem 3.12), Lemma 3.14 |
| `Examples/` | the concrete monoids: `ℕ₀ ∪ {∞}`, `ℝ≥0 ∪ ℝ̃>0 ∪ {∞}`, Diophantine monoids, the Dedekind case |
| `Modules/` | Definition 2.4 and §4: classes of modules, Theorem 4.3, projective modules, the ring examples |
| `TwoGen/` | §5 |
| `Paper/` | the paper's results in the paper's terms; nothing depends on it |

`Core/` and `Braiding/` are pure monoid theory: they mention no modules.

## Building and checking

```
lake exe cache get   # download Mathlib's prebuilt files first; building Mathlib takes hours
lake build           # checks everything
```

Lean and Mathlib are pinned to `v4.33.0`. Do not run `lake update`; Mathlib bumps go through
`.github/workflows/update.yml`.

CI builds the project on every push. It rejects any `sorry`, checks the layering, and checks that
no `axiom` is declared anywhere in the development. To see what a single result depends on, use
`#print axioms KappaMonoid.theorem_4_3` in any file that imports `KappaMonoid`.

`CLAUDE.md` has the conventions for contributors.
