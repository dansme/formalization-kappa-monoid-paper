# κ-monoids and infinite direct-sum decompositions, in Lean 4

A formalisation of

> Zahra Nazemian and Daniel Smertnig, *A monoid-theoretical approach to infinite direct-sum
> decompositions of modules* (`kappa_monoids.tex`, `kappa_monoids.pdf` in this repository).

It covers Sections 2–5 of the paper:

- **κ-monoids**: sums over families of size at most κ.
- **Braidings and universal κ-extensions**: Theorem 3.12, Proposition 3.15.
- **The κ-monoids `V^κ(C)` of classes of modules**: Theorem 4.3 and its corollaries.
- **Realisation of two-generated ℵ₀-monoids by hereditary rings**: Theorem 5.3 and Corollary 5.5.

## Status

- **Complete:** every numbered result of §§2–5 is formalised, except the few listed under "What is
  not formalised" below.
- **Also covered:** the unnumbered claims that later results rely on.
- **Sorry-free:** `lake build` checks everything.
- **No assumed theorems:** the Bergman–Dicks realisation theorem, which the paper quotes, is
  proved here, see below. Every result depends only on Lean's standard axioms (`propext`,
  `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`).
- **Pinned toolchain:** Lean and Mathlib `v4.33.0`.

## Reading it against the paper

`KappaMonoid/Paper/` is written to be read with the PDF open. Nothing else depends on it.

- `Section2.lean`, `Section3.lean`, `Section4.lean` are indices. Each paper item is followed by the
  declaration(s) that formalise it. Each file ends with what is not formalised, and why.
- `Section5.lean` restates every result of §5 in the paper's own terms. The standing assumption
  ("H is a non-cyclic ℵ₀-monoid generated by x₁, x₂") is bundled as `Setting5`.
  `Section5Extra.lean` holds the unnumbered claims of §5.
- `Definition21.lean` and `Definition218.lean` transcribe the definitions of κ-monoids and
  λ⁻-monoids word for word. Each proves that its transcription is the same structure the
  development works with.

Throughout the library, docstrings open with the paper reference (**Lemma 3.14(2)**, …), and most
carry a short `Paper proof:` paragraph.

## The Bergman–Dicks realisation theorem

| Theorem | Statement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `bergmanDicksData` | For every field `k`, every reduced commutative monoid `M` with order-unit `u` is `V(R)` for a hereditary `k`-algebra `R`, with `[R] ↦ u` | Bergman 1974, Thm 6.2; Bergman–Dicks 1978, §3 |

The paper quotes this result. It is proved in `KappaMonoid/Bergman/` (`Bergman/Realization.lean`),
and was the development's one axiom until then.

- **`V`** follows Bergman. The ring is presented by universal idempotents and isomorphisms, and `V`
  is computed one generator or relation at a time. After a matrix reduction, each step is a
  coproduct over `k^ι`. For such coproducts, the normal form, well-positioned families and
  Propositions 6.2, 8.2 and 8.4 of *Modules over coproducts of rings* (1974) give Corollaries 2.6
  and 2.8.
- **Heredity** departs from both papers. The ring is quasi-free: idempotents and isomorphisms
  lift along square-zero extensions. Quasi-free algebras over a field are hereditary. No direct
  limits are needed, so the monoid need not be finitely generated.

It is used where the paper uses it:

- Corollary 4.7(1), direction (i) ⇒ (ii), and hence Theorem 5.3's backward direction and Corollary
  5.5;
- the §5 preamble's one-generator criterion;
- Proposition 2.16, through Leavitt's theorem, which is derived from it.

## Differences from the paper

None of these changes what is proved. Each is explained in the docstring of the declaration
concerned.

- **Left modules.** Mathlib's modules are left modules, and the paper's are right modules. So the
  paper's "right hereditary" is `IsLeftHereditary` here. An unqualified "hereditary" is
  `IsHereditary`, which means both sides.
- **One notion of summation.** A κ-monoid is set up as a λ⁻-monoid with λ = κ⁺. Sums are taken
  over any index type of the right size, not over the cardinal itself. The literal transcriptions
  in `Paper/Definition21.lean` and `Paper/Definition218.lean` show that nothing changes.
- **Braidings in normal form.** Definition 3.1 fixes an arbitrary limit well-order. Here braidings
  are indexed by `ι × ℕ`, one ω-block per limit element. Lemma 3.5 (`isBraidedOn_iff_isBraided`)
  proves this is equivalent for every limit well-order.
- **Universes.** A ring, its modules and κ share one universe.
  `IsUniversalKExtension` takes the universe of its test objects as a parameter. The main results
  hold for every such universe.
- **Two hypotheses the paper leaves implicit:**
  - κ > ℵ₀ in the converse of Remark 2.19 (`LMonoid.ofCompatible`). At κ = ℵ₀ there is no infinite
    λ < κ.
  - C reduced in the §5 preamble's form `C ∪ {∞}`. The trivial extension exists only for reduced
    C.
- **Places where the formalisation proves more:**
  - Proposition 3.15(2) holds for any system whose solutions are saturated (`prop_3_15_two`).
  - Proposition 5.4's final statement holds for every ring whose projectives are direct sums of
    finitely generated ones.
  - Lemma 3.4(3) holds without its side condition.
  - For λ > ℵ₀, the two middle partitions in Lemma 3.7 can be taken equal.

## What is not formalised

- **Remark 3.18.** Background from the literature, and nothing later depends on it.
- **Corollary 4.6**, apart from the hereditary case. The other five ring classes rest on results
  quoted from the literature.
- **Examples 4.8(2) and (5)–(7).** Item (2) is a question. Items (5)–(7) rest on classical
  descriptions of `V(R)` quoted from the literature: Bass, Herbera–Příhoda, Levy–Robson.
- **Steinitz's theorem in Examples 4.8(4).** It is quoted. The monoid side is formalised in full.
- **Three unproved remarks:**
  - the remark after Corollary 4.7 (tex 1840);
  - the negative half of the caution in Examples 4.8(1);
  - the motivational remark before Proposition 2.16. This one follows from `prop_2_16_iff`.

## Classical results proved along the way

`KappaMonoid/ForMathlib/` contains results Mathlib lacks. They depend on nothing else in the
development.

- **Kaplansky's theorem**: every projective module is a direct sum of countably generated
  projectives.
- **Albrecht's theorem**: over a hereditary ring, every projective module is a direct sum of
  finitely generated projectives.
- **Invariance of infinite rank** for free modules, with no rank condition on the ring.
- **Uniqueness of multiplicities of simple summands**, including infinite multiplicities.
- **The classification of cyclic monoids**: `ℕ₀` or `C_{m,n}`.
- **Hereditary rings** (left, right, two-sided), trace ideals, and some facts on `finsum` and
  cardinal sums.

## Layout

The code is organised by subject. Each layer imports only the layers above it in this table, and
`scripts/check_layering.sh` enforces that in CI.

| Directory | Contents |
|---|---|
| `ForMathlib/` | general results, no κ-monoids |
| `Bergman/` | the Bergman–Dicks realisation theorem, no κ-monoids |
| `Core/` | §2: κ- and λ⁻-monoids, homomorphisms, free and cyclic κ-monoids, order-units |
| `Braiding/` | §3: braidings, universal κ-extensions (Theorem 3.12), Lemma 3.14 |
| `Examples/` | the concrete monoids: `ℕ₀ ∪ {∞}`, `ℝ≥0 ∪ ℝ̃>0 ∪ {∞}`, Diophantine monoids, the Dedekind case |
| `Modules/` | Definition 2.4 and §4: classes of modules, Theorem 4.3, projective modules, the ring examples |
| `TwoGen/` | §5 |
| `Paper/` | the paper's results, as above |

`Core/` and `Braiding/`, and the monoid-theoretic parts of `Examples/` and `TwoGen/`, mention no
modules.
